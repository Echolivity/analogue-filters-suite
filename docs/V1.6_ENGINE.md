# v1.6 engine: stability, fault containment, 4x–16x oversampling, Reference mode

This release puts into practice the P0 items from the independent review (`docs/independent-review/`), following the decisions you made:

| Decision | Meaning in v1.6 |
|---|---|
| "change slightly with the fix" | The solver fix applies everywhere. There is no "Classic" solver. Existing Sets that use Sherman, Steiner or the BPF SVF can sound slightly different (measured below). |
| "Reference" | New instances start in **REFERENCE** mode. Sets saved before v1.6 have no stored value for the new parameter, so Live loads its default and they open in REFERENCE too. See *Compatibility*. |
| "Optional 4x, 8x, 16x" | **High Oversampling** OFF / 4x / 8x / 16x, for the filters and TUBE. |
| "Yes" (sources / hardware) | Waiting. The research-gated items (R4–R6, R9, R10; P2/P3) are not part of this release. |

All numbers below come from `source/tests/v16_tests.py`, which runs the exact gen~ code offline (C transpile) and compares it with the v1.5 engine kept in `source/tests/baseline/dubfilter_v1.5.genexpr`. Raw data: `source/build/v16_results.json`.

## 1. Stability fix: implicit non-linearity evaluation

**Root cause, from the review.** Sherman and Steiner evaluated their non-linear gain terms (the secants) from the *previous* sample's states. With high resonance near fs/4, that one-sample lag turns into period-2 parametric pumping, so the loop gain grows without limit.

**Fix.** After the first solve, each model re-evaluates its non-linearities with the *current* sample's values and solves again (a single fixed-point step).

- **Sherman:** Rb is recomputed from the filter-2 band output.
- **Steiner:** the two stage tanh-secants and the feedback secant are recomputed from vA/vB.
- **BPF SVF:** the regeneration limiter is solved implicitly, by recomputing `bu` with the current-sample limiter gain. This was a new finding: at fc ≈ fs/4 the SVF reached +33.5 dBFS.

| Test | v1.5 | v1.6 |
|---|---|---|
| Reported failure point (48 kHz, 12 kHz, res 100 %, 1x), Sherman peak | **+249.4 dBFS** | −1.8 dBFS |
| Same point, Steiner | **+30.4 dBFS** | +3.8 dBFS |
| fs/4 sweep: 6 LPF models, ±100 cents, res 92–100 %, 44.1 + 48 kHz (756 runs), worst peak | — | +4.1 dBFS |
| fs/4 sweep: 15 BPF configurations at 44.1 kHz (330 runs), worst peak | +33.5 dBFS (SVF) | +0.2 dBFS |

HPF models cannot reach fs/4: their cutoff range ends at 2 kHz.

## 2. Fault containment

- Each filter stage checks its output every sample. If it is non-finite or |y| ≥ 1e6, the stage's state for that channel is zeroed and the dry signal is passed for that sample.
- The final output stage and the DC blocker have the same guard.
- `out17` raises a fault flag.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| A single 1e9 input sample | Contained (flag raised); output exactly 0 ten ms later |
| 12 heavy configurations in normal use (res 0/100 %, Reference) | **0 activations** |

## 3. Oversampling 1x / 2x / 4x / 8x / 16x

The 2x half-band stage (HIIR polyphase allpass, about 106 dB rejection) is now cascaded up to 16x. The 2x path is bit-identical to v1.5. A factor change uses the existing duck and reset (4 ms down, reset, 4 ms up).

TUBE follows the same factor (minimum 2x) and has its own cascade. Its dry path (Mix) is phase-matched through a matching down-cascade, so it stays flat within 0.07 dB at 2x, 4x and 16x.

## 4. Reference / Creative

- **REFERENCE** (default): effective factor = max(your setting, the minimum of every active model). The minimums are measured, not estimated. Each is the lowest factor at which the model's aliasing is ≤ −80 dBc at the bright test point:
  - LPF: SEM 1x; Sherman, K35 and 303 2x; Ladder and Steiner 4x
  - HPF: 2x for all models
  - BPF: 4x for all architectures
  - TUBE: 8x
- **CREATIVE:** the engine runs at exactly your setting, including 1x, so 1x aliasing is available as a sound.

The ENGINE strip shows the factors the filters and TUBE are actually running at (gen~ outlets 15 and 16).

Every HPF model and BPF configuration in Reference mode measures ≤ −80 dBc; the worst is HPF Steiner at −89.9 dBc.

## 4b. OPTO from the Teletronix schematic (added after you supplied the LA-2A manuals)

**Sources:**
- the Teletronix LA-2A operating manual (3-66) with its 11-66 addendum
- the UREI reissue manual (1992), which reprints both, with Lawrence's 1964 JSMPTE paper and schematic C-10953 rev C (6 Aug 1968)

Full analysis: [LA2A_MANUAL_CHECK.md](independent-review/LA2A_MANUAL_CHECK.md).

**What the schematic shows.**
- T1 → R6 68 kΩ → R7 (1–2.7 kΩ) → the T4 photocells to ground. Peak Reduction (R2) is fed from the R6/R7 junction, so detection is **feedback**.
- SW3 shorts R7 in COMP. In LIMIT the detector sees the cell node × (1 + R7/R_cell).
- R37 ∥ C12 sits between V3 (via C9) and the balance pot R3, loaded by C6/R30.

**What v1.6 implements.** These changes apply to the internal detector only; CHAIN and EXT keys keep their previous gain law in both modes (bit-identical with Emphasis at 0 %; the R37 network replaces the old shelf for every source).
- **Gain law:** G = 1/(1 + q), q = (1.6·c)^p, floored at 40 dB of reduction (manual: "up to 40 dB").
- **COMP:** p = 2. Bit-identical to before, below the 40 dB floor.
- **LIMIT:** p = 3.5, plus the R7 sense term (1 + 0.0444·q), with R7 = 1.8 kΩ.
- **Why two exponents.** Both published curves describe switch-era units: the addendum gives Figure 1 as the *limiter* curve, and gives COMP as about 3:1. No single cell exponent fits both (see the table below). By your decision, each mode follows its own published curve. A single physical cell could not do this.
- **Emphasis:** the R37 network (3rd-order bilinear, R37 = 0–1 MΩ).

| Cell exponent | LIMIT: output rise at +5/10/15/30 dB above breakaway (Figure 1: 3.4/5.1/5.6/5.7) | COMP: ratio at 6/12/18 dB GR (addendum ≈ 3:1; "100 % → 30 %" ≈ 2.6:1) |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3.6/6.5/8.9/13.7 | **2.2/2.6/2.8** ← COMP |
| 3 | 3.0/5.1/6.6/8.0 | 2.8/3.4/3.7 |
| 3.5 | **2.8/4.7/5.9/6.0** ← LIMIT | 3.2/3.8/4.2 |

These values come from the static solve with the schematic values (`tests/review/la2a_model/gam.py`).

**LIMIT, before vs after, on identical metrics.** 1 kHz; output rise in dB above breakaway; sweep from −70 dBFS so breakaway is captured at every Peak Reduction setting (`tests/review/la2a_before_after.py`, `la2a_dip.py`):

| | +5 dB | +10 dB | +15 dB | +30 dB | After the flat top |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Figure 1 | 3.4 | 5.1 | 5.6 | 5.7 | (stops at about 25 dB GR) |
| Before (PR 80) | 1.2 | 1.8 | 2.5 | 4.0 | keeps rising (about 2 dB per 20 dB) |
| After (any PR; the shape is independent of Peak Reduction) | 3.2 | 4.7 | 6.2 | 7.1 | **dips about 5 dB** toward the 40 dB floor |

The worst deviation from Figure 1 is 1.4 dB. LIMIT rounds into a flat top about 15–25 dB above breakaway (peak +7.3 dB above breakaway at about 20 dB GR). It then **dips about 5 dB** as reduction deepens toward the 40 dB cell floor, after which output rises 1:1. Figure 1 stops at about 25 dB GR and shows no dip; the dip comes from the R7 term with the steeper exponent. R7 = 1.0 kΩ, the low end of the schematic's range, would cut it to about 1.9 dB, at the cost of a 3.0 dB worst Figure 1 deviation instead of 1.4.
**Other measurements:**

| | Before | After | Manual |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programme material, PR 100: GR reversals (5 ms grid) | 577 (hunting); max GR 77 dB | 110; max GR 40 dB (floor) | — |
| LIMIT THD at ≥ 30 dB GR | 1.8 % at 100 Hz | 100 Hz 0.66 %, 200 Hz 0.17 %, 400 Hz 0.04 %, 1 kHz 0.007 % | < 0.5 % (setup uses 400 or 1000 Hz) |
| Emphasis at max, side-chain sensitivity | HF shelf above 2.5 kHz | 15 kHz +12 dB vs 500 Hz, +8 dB vs 1 kHz | ≈ +10 dB vs below 1 kHz |
| Emphasis at max, GR difference 15 kHz − 500 Hz (LIMIT, 0 dBFS) | +8.2 to +8.9 dB | +12.5 to +14 dB (the stiffer loop amplifies the same shift) | ≈ +10 dB |

Emphasis measures the same at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.

**Effect on existing Sets:**
- **LIMIT with the internal detector** now has a softer knee: about 3.9:1 at PR 50, where the old law was much harder just past breakaway. It goes flat from about 15–25 dB of reduction, then dips about 5 dB toward the 40 dB floor at extreme settings.
- **Emphasis above 0 %** now *cuts* the lows going to the detector (up to about −15 dB near 200 Hz) instead of boosting the highs.
- **COMP** is unchanged below 40 dB of reduction.
- **CHAIN/EXT keys** keep their gain law in both modes; only Emphasis above 0 % changes for them.

**Assumptions:**
- R7 = 1.8 kΩ. The schematic gives a range, selected per cell.
- V3 output impedance = 35 kΩ. The result moves by < 1 dB between 25 and 50 kΩ.
- The C6/R30 trimmer is left out (< 0.5 dB effect).
- R37's position follows drawing rev C. The 3-66 text puts it ahead of R2; the two agree above 500 Hz.

**Not addressed:**
- Attack calibration. The UREI notice says the original "10 µs" should read milliseconds, consistent with OPTO's 2–8 ms. Lawrence also gives 50–100 µs "if no previous reduction", but no cell transfer data exists.
- The audio path (R5).

## 7. Known risks

- **Not yet compiled in Max.** The GenExpr grew from about 6,500 to about 14,000 lines (25 functions, 1,209 History states). The offline transpile checks syntax and scoping, but Max's gen~ compiler may take noticeably longer to compile, and it has not been tested at this size. Please open the device once and report compile time and any errors.
- The `for` loop in the oversampled filter body uses GenExpr's documented `for (i = 0; i < n; i += 1)` form.
- High-frequency self-oscillation in Sherman and Steiner is a little lower in level than in v1.5 (see §1). This is the measured consequence of the fix, not a tuning choice. It can be revisited once primary sources are available.
